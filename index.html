Designed for the UNICEF and WFP study in Katsina, 2026

Hausa interview recordings, turned into checked English transcripts

The joint operation plans 131 interviews and group discussions across Dutsin-Ma, Safana and Kafur. Done by hand, every recording means hours of typing and translating, and the picture across interviews only comes together once the team has left the field.

I designed a system that does the routine part automatically and keeps a person on the step that needs judgement: checking the Hausa before anything is translated.

Field supervisorThe system
  1. 1Field supervisorUpload

    Uploads the recording to their area's folder in Google Drive.

  2. 2The systemTranscribe

    Gemini transcribes the Hausa in five-minute sections, in parallel, with a time on every line.

  3. 3Field supervisorCheck

    Corrects the draft against the recording, fills in the interview details and marks it ready. Nothing is translated before this.

  4. 4The systemTranslate

    Produces the English translation with a summary, key findings, themes and key statements.

  5. 5The systemShare

    Sends the documents to the supervisor and lets the study team know the translation is ready.

  6. 6The systemDigest

    Every evening, a numbered summary of coverage, themes and quotes by area, building on the one before. A separate copy for the field managers adds coaching points for the team.

Around the edges

  • Gives every interview an ID by area and logs each recording and who checked it.
  • Reminds supervisors about drafts they have not yet checked.
  • Takes typed notes from interviews that could not be recorded, and summarises and translates them too.
  • Labels speakers by role rather than name, and shares transcripts view-only.

What it changed

  • Minutes per interview instead of hours of typing and translating.
  • A person checks every Hausa transcript before it is translated, and every line carries its time in the recording, so any quote can be traced back to the audio.
  • Emerging issues reach the study team, UNICEF and WFP during fieldwork, not weeks after it.
  • Recordings stay in the project's own Google Drive.

Other systems I have built

Each one replaced work someone was doing by hand. I write the guides that let colleagues run them without me.

Data collection for 158 organisations

Built for the impact investment study. It sends each organisation its invitation and reminders at day 5, day 12 and day 22, writes every status back to the contact sheet, reads each reply as it arrives, rates how complete it is, and sends either a thank-you or a request for what is missing.

Opportunity Radar

Replaces hours of checking donor and government tender websites by hand. I catalogued 184 sources across 111 websites by how each can be accessed, and ranked them by how many real opportunities they had produced. The radar monitors 70 or more of them on a schedule, removes duplicates, filters and scores what it finds, and sends a weekly summary to four team leads.

Two AI models and a rules-based scorer run side by side, and their results are compared before anything is sent, so no single model decides what reaches a colleague.

Field team contracting

Built for the Katsina field team. Team members submit their details on a form and sign their contract online, and signed copies and the payment list go to management and Accounts automatically.

Reading documents at scale

Python systems that read several hundred annual reports and financial documents and turn them into clean datasets, including matching and merging around 983 records from 35 separate spreadsheets.

How I work

The FRAME Method is the model I designed for taking on any assignment, from framing the question to handing over work that runs without me.

FFrame the question

Agree the real question and what evidence would answer it, before committing to a method.

RRules and design

Fix definitions, classification rules and design decisions before the work starts.

AAcquire the evidence

Build and gather it hands on: instruments, fieldwork, primary sources.

MMeasure and verify

Analyse, then check each result against its source and flag confidence explicitly.

EEmbed for use

Document and automate so the work runs again without me, and translate it for whoever must act.

  • Every number traced. Each figure carries its source and a confidence grade, and a figure that fails its check stops the analysis instead of reaching a chart.
  • Corrected in the open. On a published report I worked through a 22-point outside review, checked every point against the source, and reported a finding as no longer holding when a stricter test showed it did not survive.
  • Ethics built in. Consent and confidentiality in every study, field teams trained on consent and child protection, and national ethics approval and PEPFAR data quality standards on health studies.
